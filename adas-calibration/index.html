ADAS Camera Calibration in Toronto

If your car has a camera behind the windshield, it needs recalibrating after a new windshield goes in.

See 17 shops that do this

What ADAS is

ADAS stands for advanced driver-assistance systems. Many newer vehicles have a camera behind the windshield, near the rear-view mirror. It feeds lane departure warning, lane keeping, automatic emergency braking and traffic sign recognition.

Why calibration matters

The camera is attached to the windshield. When the glass is replaced, the camera moves. If it is even slightly off, safety features may not work as they should. Transport Canada has said owner's manuals for these vehicles state the camera must be recalibrated after windshield replacement.

How to tell if your car has a windshield camera

  1. Look behind the rear-view mirror from inside the car.
  2. A plastic housing with a small forward-facing lens is usually the camera.
  3. Check your owner's manual for "lane keeping" or "pre-collision".

Types of calibration

  • Static: in a shop, with the vehicle in front of special targets.
  • Dynamic: by driving on roads with clear lane markings while a tool reads the camera.
  • Both: some vehicles need a combination.

Questions to ask

  • Do you calibrate in-house, or send it elsewhere?
  • Is calibration in the replacement price?
  • Will I get a report showing it was done?

General information, not legal or insurance advice. Coverage depends on your policy.

Common questions

Do I need calibration after a chip repair?

Usually not, because the glass and camera stay in place.

What if I skip it?

Lane keeping, emergency braking or other assistance features may not work correctly, sometimes without any warning light.
